🛡️ Your Opponent's Move Filters

These settings determine which of your opponent's moves BookBuilder will include in your repertoire.
BookBuilder will calculate the likelihood of your opponent's move being played in your games. Increasing the second number here will therefore increase the size of your repertoire. For example, if you select 1 in 500 games, BookBuilder will prepare a repertoire with all your opponent's replies that are likely to occur more than once per 500 games you play, according to the data from your Lichess Database settings. This 1 in X games number assumes you are always playing with white or black. It includes the probability of reaching the starting position of your repertoire, even if that position is fairly deep (eg Najdorf). BookBuilder assumes that the moves you play are always chosen and so do not affect the probability of reaching a position.
Moves in the Lichess Database played less times than this will not be included in your repertoire. You don't want to be generating likely lines from tiny amounts of games.

🎯 Your Candidate Move Selectors

These settings determine how BookBuilder will select repertoire moves it recommends you play. BookBuilder will recommend you play the highest winrate moves within these constraints.
This determines how many of the most played moves in a position BookBuilder should consider. If you select 5, BookBuilder will ignore strong moves that aren't in the top 5 most played in a position.
%
If a move was played less often than this percentage in a given position, we won't consider it as a candidate move.
If a move was played in fewer games than this in a given position, we won't consider it as a candidate move.
%
The higher this confidence level, the more conservative BookBuilder becomes in move selection. Higher confidence requires moves to have more data and better statistical evidence before being selected. This sets the statistical confidence interval (95% = α=0.05) for evaluating move quality.
By default, draws count as half a point for winrate calculations. But you may want a 'must win' repertoire, where a draw is as good as a loss. If so, untick this.

🤖 Engine Filters

These determine how BookBuilder will use engine analysis when generating your repertoire. Note that high engine depths can significantly increase the length of time BookBuilder takes to make a repertoire.
If this is off, the engine will not be used to filter your candidate moves.
This is the depth the engine will use when evaluating moves. The recommended minimum is 20 for stable evaluations in the opening phase.
If there is an opponent continuation we wish to consider, but we don't have sufficient data for our reply according to our filters, we can use the engine to generate the best reply until there are no opponent continuations that meet our criteria.
The maximum engine evaluation we are willing to accept, provided the winrate is better (-300 = losing by 3 pawns in evaluation). We never give up a forced mate, of course.

📄 PGN Output Configuration

Choose how you want your chess repertoire to be formatted in the final PGN output.
How should multiple lines be presented in your repertoire?
Creates separate PGN entries for each line like:
[Event "Sicilian Defense Line 1"]
1. e4 c5 2. Nf3 d6
{Move playrates: ...}
Combines related lines into a single PGN with branches like:
[Event "Sicilian Defense Tree"]
1. e4 c5 (1...e5 2. Nf3) 2. Nf3 d6 3. d4
{Combined statistics: ...}
All repertoires include detailed move statistics and playrates in a statistics block after each line.
Statistics Format:
1. e4 c5 2. Nf3 d6
{Move playrates:
+45.67%    c5
+78.23%    d6
Line winrate: +52.4% over 15,234 games}
💡 Tip
Individual + End Block: Best for studying separate lines in depth
Tree + End Block: Great for understanding how lines relate to each other
Individual + Inline: Compact format showing move-by-move statistics
Tree + Inline: Advanced format combining tree structure with inline stats
